A07 - Bracket FEA

Objective

The objective is to use finite element analysis to improve the bracket from A05 and A06 so it is stiffer, stronger, and lighter than the original. The workflow has an outer loop and an inner loop. The inner loop validates each result: set up a static stress study, solve, read the maximum stress, maximum displacement, and safety factor, then refine the mesh until the results agree within 7% (or stop after the third attempt and keep the finest mesh). The outer loop uses the validated results to change the geometry, adding material where the part is weak and removing it where it carries little load, and it runs three times. The material stays Aluminum 6061 throughout.

Material and Load Case

Aluminum 6061, unchanged from A05 and A06 (6061-T6 (SS) in the SolidWorks library: E = 10,007,604 psi, Sy = 39,885 psi, density 0.0975 lb/in3).

Load. 1,600 lbf total, applied downward on the cylinder's lower surface. This is the same W = 2F from A05, where each side of the strap carries F = 800 lbf.

Constraint. Fixed geometry on the interior faces of the T-slot, which is where the bracket hangs from the T-beam.

Design Change

Design I fails by a wide margin, with a safety factor of 0.18 against a target of 4. Almost all of the stress sits in the connecting plate (Feature B), while the block around the T-slot stays nearly unstressed. The A05 hand calculations treated the plate as an axial bar. In the model, the cylinder hangs to one side of the plate, so the 1,600 lbf load acts offset from the plate and bends it, which the axial model did not include. The 0.17 in thick plate carries that bending, and it deflects about 0.46 in at the cylinder.

For Design II I added material where the stress is: I thickened the plate with a 0.2 in extrude and added 0.1 in fillets at the junctions of Features A and B and of Features B and C, to reduce the stress concentration where the plate meets the cylinder and the block. The unstressed block is the place to remove material, which I plan to do for Design III.

Results

Mistakes

  • Mass reported with no material assigned. The first Mass Properties reading for Design I gave 0.17 lb at a density of 0.04 lb/in3. 6061-T6 is 0.0975 lb/in3, so the part had no material assigned in SolidWorks and fell back to the default density of 1 g/cm3. The study had the correct material, so the stress, displacement, and safety factor results were not affected. Assigning 6061-T6 to the part fixes the mass.
  • Unreadable factor of safety plot. The default legend ran up to 1016, which turned the whole part red and hid where the part actually fails. Limiting the plot to areas below a factor of safety of 1 makes the failing region visible.
