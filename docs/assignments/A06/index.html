A06 - Parametric Bracket and Drawing

Objective

The objective is to take the five hand-calculated dimensions from A05 (the bracket that holds the strap and hangs off the T-beam) and rebuild them as a fully parametric SolidWorks model. Every dimension on every feature is tied to a global variable instead of a typed number, so the whole part updates on its own if the load, the material, or the T-beam's fit tolerances ever change. After the model, I laid out a fully dimensioned multiview drawing with the fit tolerances from the T-beam called out on the mating dimensions.

Global Variables and Equations

Before modeling anything, I set up every load, material property, and feature dimension as a global variable in the Equations manager. The load and material values feed the strength equation for each feature, which feeds the dimension, so nothing downstream is a typed-in number.

A correction partway through: I had originally mapped the T-beam's fit dimensions onto the T-slot wrong, which gave the slot's chamber a width of 1.5 in. Going back to the T-beam drawing, the chamber actually has to clear the full flange width, 2(0.9992) + 0.498 ≈ 2.5 in, so C moved from 1.5 in to 2.5 in. That one change rippled through M_C, Z_C, and t_C on its own, and through E_len, M_E, Z_E, and t_E too, since E is sized off of C. I didn't have to touch any of those by hand.

Lessons Learned

Time spent: 3 hours.

The equation that drove the most important dimension was the strength equation on Feature C, the T-slot's bottom wall. t_C comes from t_C = (6 · Z_C / w) ^ (1/2), where Z_C = n · M_C / Sy and M_C = P · C / 4. I tied all three of those into the Equations manager as expressions, not as a number I calculated by hand and typed into the extrude. That decision mattered once I caught the mapping error on the T-slot's chamber width: when I corrected C from 1.5 in to 2.5 in, M_C, Z_C, and t_C all recalculated by themselves, and so did E_len, M_E, Z_E, and t_E downstream of it. If I had typed t_C in as a plain 0.621 in instead of an equation, I would have had to redo that calculation by hand and re-enter the extrude depth myself, and I likely would have missed updating Feature E's numbers since they depend on C two steps removed.

For tolerances, I put the tightest class on the 1.0000 in dimension that mates with the T-beam's b fit (+0.0010/−0.0008 in), since that's a sliding interface where too much clearance lets the bracket wobble on the beam and too little stops it from sliding on at all. I used the loosest class, the default two-place ±.01, on the 2.67 in overall length, since that dimension doesn't touch another part and only affects how the bracket looks sitting on the shelf. If I'd put the tightest tolerance on every dimension instead of just the mating ones, every cut on this part would need a tighter process than a mill with a standard end mill can hold economically, which raises cost for no functional reason on surfaces nothing else touches.
