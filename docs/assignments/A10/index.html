A10 – Lead Screw Translating System

Objective

This is part one of a two-part team assignment with Ethan King: the conceptual design of a purely mechanical tensile tester. The machine pulls a specimen with up to 2 kN of axial tension while a crosshead moves at a controllable 1–50 mm/min, and it has no electronics and no software. Two identical lead screws, one on each side of the frame, move the crosshead, and the design safety factor is 4. Part one has six pieces of work: size the lead screws (diameter, rotational speed, and torque) and select them, select a bronze nut and flange, design the crosshead for strength and for a maximum deflection of 0.1 mm, tabulate the cost of purchased materials, and assemble the machine in CAD with the frame, screws, nuts, and crosshead.

The 250 MPa yield strength sits between two published values for 1018 steel: 240 MPa for hot rolled 1018 (MakeItFrom) and 370 MPa for cold drawn 1018 in the Elgin data sheet. That data sheet lists Brinell 126, the same hardness McMaster-Carr lists for the selected screw. The minimum diameter in section 1 would be 4.61 mm at 240 MPa, so the choice of value does not change the screw selection.

1. Minimum Leadscrew Diameter: Ethan King

i. Knowns and unknowns. Knowns: F = 2 kN = 450 lbf, n = 4, Sy = 250 MPa, two screws. Unknowns: load per screw Fs, allowable stress σallow, minimum area Amin, and minimum diameter dmin.

ii. Free body diagram. One leadscrew, loaded in axial tension by Fs = F/2 through the nut, with the frame bearing supplying the equal and opposite reaction.

iii. Model and algebraic solution.

  • Load per screw: Fs = F/2.
  • Allowable stress: σallow = Sy/n.
  • Axial stress on a round bar, σ = Fs/A with A = πd2/4. At the minimum size σ = σallow, so Amin = Fs/σallow and dmin = √(4Fs/(πσallow)).

iv. Numerical solution.

v. Answer. Each leadscrew must be at least 4.52 mm (0.178 in) in diameter.

2. Range of Rotational Speed: Ethan King

i. Knowns and unknowns. Knowns: crosshead speed v = 1 to 50 mm/min and lead L = 0.25 in = 6.35 mm per revolution. Unknowns: rotational speed N at each end of the range.

ii. Free body diagram. This is a motion problem and no force enters it, so the sketch is kinematic: the screw turns at N and the nut and crosshead advance at v.

iii. Model and algebraic solution. The nut advances one lead per revolution, so v = N·L and N = v/L. The lead is the travel per turn, which is the number of thread starts times the pitch. The selected screw has two starts at a pitch of 0.125 in, so its lead is 0.25 in.

iv. Numerical solution.

Nmin1/6.350.157 rpm
Nmax50/6.357.87 rpm

v. Answer. Both screws turn at the same speed, from 0.157 rpm to 7.87 rpm (crosshead speed 0.0394 to 1.969 in/min).

3. Maximum Torque: Ethan King

i. Knowns and unknowns. Knowns: Fs = 1000 N per screw, lead L = 6.35 mm, μ = 0.15, and a mean thread diameter dm. Unknown: the torque T that one screw needs to raise the load. The mean diameter depends on the screw that is selected, so this section gives a first pass with the 4.6 mm minimum diameter from section 1, and section 4 repeats the calculation for the selected screw.

ii. Free body diagram. One screw with the axial load Fs acting on the thread at the mean radius dm/2, the thread friction force μN opposing the motion, and the driving torque T about the screw axis.

iii. Model and algebraic solution. Torque to raise the load on a power screw: T = (Fsdm/2) · (L + πμdm)/(πdm − μL).

iv. Numerical solution (first pass, dm = 4.6 mm).

v. Answer. This is a lower bound: 4.6 mm is the smallest rod that carries the load, and a real screw has a larger mean diameter and therefore needs more torque. The design torque comes from the selected screw in section 4.

4. Leadscrew Selection: Ethan King

The selected screw is McMaster-Carr 99030A073: a 1018 carbon steel precision Acme lead screw, fast-travel, right-hand, 3/4 in–8 thread, 3 ft long, 2:1 speed ratio, with two thread starts and 0.25 in of travel per turn. It costs $51.44 each and two are needed. Both screws are identical.

Torque for the Selected Screw

For a 3/4 in–8 Acme thread the mean (pitch) diameter is dm = d − p/2 = 0.75 − 0.125/2 = 0.6875 in = 17.46 mm, using the basic Acme thread form. With the same formula as in section 3:

Fsdm/21000(0.017463)/28.73 N·m

T (square-thread formula)8.73(0.2705)2.36 N·m
T (Acme, μ′ = μ/cos 14.5° = 0.155)8.73(0.2756)2.41 N·m

The Acme thread has a 29° included angle, so each flank sits 14.5° from the plane perpendicular to the screw axis, which raises the effective friction coefficient to μ/cos 14.5°. The design torque is 2.41 N·m per screw (1.78 lbf·ft), about 4.8 N·m for both screws. This counts thread friction only; friction in the thrust bearing at the end of each screw adds to it.
