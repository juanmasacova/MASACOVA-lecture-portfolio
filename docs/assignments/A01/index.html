Task A
In this section I am asked to find two portfolios, and analyze them.

Navigability
In terms of navigability, both of the portfolios are excellent. Right in the home page of the first one there is a big button that takes you to their work, probably took me 2 seconds to get to the projects page. In the second portfolio the homepage itself is the project showcase, which makes it, if possible, easier than the first one. They both have menus where you can find contact information and other useful information about them.

Reproducibility
Both of these portfolios serve more as a showcase of what they have done, than a explanation of how to reproduce their work. Their project pages feel more like a trophy case than a scientific journal. It would take a lot of questions to be able to replicate their work, but it does give details about each project to the point where you could have a good idea of their work and the scope of the projects. Honestly I don't think either of them was aiming to explain exactly how they did any of their projects, rather just show them.

Evidence of reasoning
I think both portfolios showcase a great amount of reasoning, I mean, their achievements aren't small...and they seem to know what they are talking about. I do believe the second portfolio does a better job at telling a story of how the project came to be, and with that, a clearer picture of the reasoning. I would say the first one is more focused on the accomplishment itself.

Professional tone
I do think both portfolios are extremely professional in their tone. The structure, length and tone follow all rules of professionalism. They use short paragraphs with very dense information about what they specifically did in every project. They are focused on the things a company would like to hear, skip informalities and information that might be irrelevant for a recruiter

Sean McConoughey's portfolio functions as a concise presentation of his technical experience, with a structured layout that allows a reader to move quickly between projects, experience, skills, and achievements. The best aspect of the portfolio is its use of specific engineering information and results, such as thrust values, data-acquisition rates, and detection performance, which gives me measurable evidence of his contributions. Project descriptions also identify relevant methods and technologies, providing some insight into how engineering problems were approached rather than only displaying final results. However, the documentation generally remains at a summary level. Overall, the portfolio is well suited for communicating technical experience to an employer, but could function more effectively as engineering documentation by including more of the calculations, design decisions, and verification processes behind each project.

Katie Heinemann's portfolio is a very strong engineering portfolio because it prioritizes technical projects and provides a lot of context to understand her role and contributions. The project-based organization she uses makes individual work easy to locate, while each project includes descriptions of the problem, technologies used, constraints, and results. The strongest aspect is the evidence of engineering reasoning; for example, she explains how constraints such as underwater communication and network reliability affected the architecture of her IoT platform. The portfolio also includes quantitative results, like reductions in CI/CD costs and deployment times. This portfolio also fails reproducibility: although the documentation identifies technologies and design decisions, most projects do not include enough source code, design files, procedures, or detailed implementation information for another engineer to independently recreate the work. Overall, the portfolio functions effectively as evidence of engineering experience and decision-making, but it is better as a presentation of engineering work than as complete technical documentation.

Task B
Here I am tasked with analyzing a simple mechanical product with 3 components or less. I decided to go with the paper clip.

The function of this product is to spring to its original flat state, this way creating force against anything wedged into it, in this case sheets of paper. I would say the governing model of this object is the beam bending equation (σ=Mc/I) where σ = bending stress, M = bending moment, c = distance from neutral axis and I = second moment of area of the wire cross-section. I believe this model works because the structure of the clip serves as a beam that when bent creates stress. It's elastic properties counteract the stress and is there where the product is useful.

The geometry of this part allows it to sit flat and to compress anything that wedges in between the two faces of the clip. the geometry also creates a larger surface area to hold on to the paper, making it that wat more secure.

The patent number of this object is US581901A, invented by WARREN W. COLE. Some other solutions are a binder and a staple. One design decision that I really like about this object is that it is made from a single extruded piece and simply bent into shape from that first extrusion, it makes the product extremely cheap and simple to manufacture.

Decide

  1. Homepage Identity

    I believe the first thing a visitor should know is "What are they visiting". It is organized in a way that the first thing a visitor sees is the answer to that specific question. Later as they go down in the home page they will be able to have more context about exactly what topics, projects, or tools I will be using to fill this portfolio. The standard I hold for this portfolio is that the spacing and size of the pages will be consistent and comfortable to read. I decided to limit the content of my homepage a lot because there is no need to overpopulate the home page with information that will be in other pages of the website, so it just gives a great overview of what you can find in the portfolio.

  2. One Intentional Customization

    I decided to change the color scheme from red to green. I intentionally did this because this is a school portfolio, and as so it should reflect the branding of the institution. I believe this is important because I write almost as though this was a journal, and having the portfolio be the color scheme of the school serves as a reminder to stay formal and scholarly.

  3. My Documentation Standard

    As mentioned earlier I believe spacing is very important when trying to read large amounts of text, specially if it's technical, so I will hold myself to the standard of using 4 spaces after each section, 2 between tasks of the same section and a singular space from paragraph to paragraph.
